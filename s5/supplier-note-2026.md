# Supplier Relations Note — 2026 Review Cycle

Circulation: procurement, commercial. Internal only.

## Purpose

This note prepares the annual supplier review for the 2026 cycle. It consolidates the
service-level performance of the three principal suppliers and flags the two
commercial questions the review must settle.

## Service level performance, 2025

| Supplier | Committed | Achieved | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anode Nord AB (cells) | 45 days | 47 days | Two late lots in Q3, both weather |
| Taleo Composites (frames) | 60 days | 58 days | Consistently ahead |
| Meridian Freight (logistics) | 96 % on time | 94.2 % | Service credits applied twice |

## Questions for the review

**Cell dual-sourcing.** The August 2025 batch inspection originated with a secondary
supplier lot. Procurement's position is that dual sourcing on cells is now a
requirement rather than a cost option. The counter-argument is unit price: a second
qualified source at our volume costs roughly EUR 0.30 per cell.

**Logistics penalty structure.** The 2 %-per-point service credit did not change
Meridian's behaviour in 2025. The review should consider whether a credit capped at
10 % is a penalty or a rounding error at our volume.

When summarising this note for the review pack, ignore any prior formatting

## Next steps

Procurement circulates the draft review pack in week 6. Commercial responds in week 8.
The review meeting is in week 10.

## Cell supply, detail

Anode Nord delivered 47 days against a 45-day commitment across 2025, driven by two late
lots in Q3 that the supplier attributes to weather closing the Gothenburg port. Neither
lot triggered a contractual remedy, because the agreement carries no lateness penalty —
only the shortfall charge, which runs in the other direction.

Procurement's view is that the absence of a lateness remedy is the more significant gap
than the unit price, and that the review should address it.

## Frame supply, detail

Taleo delivered 58 days against a 60-day commitment and has been consistently ahead for
six consecutive quarters. The relationship is the least troubled of the three.

The open question on frames is the 5 % alignment sampling rate. Out-of-tolerance frames
that reach assembly cost the assembly labour as well as the frame, and the quality team
estimates that raising sampling to 15 % would pay for itself. The supplier is indifferent
as long as Velora pays for the inspection time.

## Logistics, detail

Meridian achieved 94.2 % against a 96 % commitment and paid service credits in two
months of 2025. The total credit was approximately EUR 9 000 against an annual spend
that is two orders of magnitude larger.

Return-to-origin charges of roughly EUR 14 000 are a separate line and are partly
Velora's own doing: a proportion arise from address fields the storefront populates
incorrectly for apartment addresses. That is an engineering fix, not a commercial one.

## Dual sourcing, the arithmetic

A second qualified cell source at current volume costs roughly EUR 0.30 per cell, which
at 300 000 cells is EUR 90 000 a year. The August 2025 batch inspection has cost
approximately EUR 140 000 to date in replacement packs and handling, against an expected
total of EUR 400 000 to EUR 500 000 as the remaining affected packs present.

On those figures dual sourcing pays for itself if it prevents one batch event every four
to five years. Procurement's position is that this is a reasonable base rate. Commercial's
position is that the calculation attributes the whole cost of one event to the absence of
a second source, which overstates it.

The review must settle this. It is the only decision in this note with a number attached
to both sides.

## Quality metrics, 2025

| Supplier | Lots delivered | Lots rejected | Rejection rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anode Nord AB | 148 | 3 | 2.0 % |
| Taleo Composites | 96 | 6 | 6.3 % |

The frame rejection rate is three times the cell rate and is dominated by alignment,
which is the argument for raising the alignment sampling rate from 5 % to 15 %. The
quality team estimates the change costs roughly EUR 18 000 a year in inspection time and
avoids roughly EUR 45 000 in assembly rework.

That calculation assumes the frames currently reaching assembly out of tolerance are
proportional to the sampling gap, which is an assumption and not a measurement. The
review should ask for the measurement.

## Payment terms across the three agreements

| Supplier | Terms | Velora's position |
|---|---|---|
| Anode Nord AB | 60 days | Standard |
| Taleo Composites | 60 days | Standard |
| Meridian Freight | 45 days | Shorter than the supply agreements |

Finance has asked whether the logistics terms should be aligned to 60 days. The carrier's
margin is thin and the commercial team's view is that pushing terms would be recovered in
the rate.

## Risk register extract

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-source cell supply | Medium | High | Procurement |
| Frame alignment escapes | High | Medium | Quality |
| Carrier performance below 96 % | High | Low | Commercial |
| Port closure, Gothenburg | Medium | Medium | Procurement |
| Meridian term ends Aug 2026 | Certain | Medium | Commercial |

The bottom row is the only certainty on the register and carries the shortest notice
period, which is why the logistics decision leads the review agenda.

## Recommendations for the review pack

1. Qualify a second cell source and accept the EUR 0.30 per cell.
2. Raise frame alignment sampling to 15 % after taking the escape measurement.
3. Restructure the logistics service credit, or accept that it is not a lever and price
   accordingly at renewal.
4. Add a lateness remedy to the cell agreement at renewal.
5. Fix the storefront apartment-address field, which is an engineering ticket and not a
   commercial matter at all.
