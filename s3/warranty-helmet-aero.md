# Aero Helmet Warranty Terms

**Effective date: 1 March 2025.**

## Scope

These terms apply to the Velora Aero helmet range and to accessory protective
equipment sold under the Velora brand.

## Coverage period

Velora covers the helmet against manufacturing defects for **24 months from the date
of delivery**. No usage-count limit applies to protective equipment.

## What is covered

- Delamination of the shell from the liner.
- Failure of the retention system or buckle under normal use.
- Defects in the fit adjustment mechanism.

## What is not covered

- A helmet that has sustained an impact. Protective equipment is single-impact by
  design; a helmet involved in a crash must be replaced regardless of visible damage,
  and this is not a warranty case.
- Cosmetic wear to the shell finish.
- Damage from solvents, or from storage above 60 °C.

## Replacement policy

Where a claim is accepted and the model is no longer produced, Velora supplies the
nearest current equivalent at no charge. There is no cash alternative.

## Crash replacement

Separately from the warranty, Velora offers a crash replacement discount of 30 % on a
new helmet within 24 months of the original purchase, on presentation of the original
order reference. This is a commercial gesture and not a warranty entitlement.

## Certification and standards

The Aero range is certified to EN 1078, the European standard for helmets for pedal
cyclists. Certification is to the standard in force at the date of manufacture; a
subsequent revision of the standard does not retroactively affect a helmet already
supplied.

Certification covers the helmet as supplied. Any modification — drilling, painting with
a solvent-based paint, adhesive mounts fixed to the shell — voids both the certification
and the warranty, because the shell and liner are engineered as a single energy-absorbing
structure and a hole in the shell changes how it deforms.

## Accessory mounts

Velora supplies a strap-mounted light bracket and a strap-mounted camera mount. Neither
attaches to the shell. Mounts that adhere to or penetrate the shell are not supplied by
Velora and their use voids coverage.

## Claim procedure

Claims are opened through the support channel with the order reference. For protective
equipment, Velora asks for photographs of the shell, the liner and the retention system
before requesting return of the item, because the majority of reported defects on
inspection turn out to be impact damage.

Where photographs show impact damage, support states plainly that the item is not a
warranty case and offers the crash replacement discount. Where they are inconclusive,
the helmet is returned at Velora's cost for assessment.

## Fit-related complaints

A helmet reported as uncomfortable or as sitting incorrectly is a fit issue and not a
defect. The return window applies in the normal way for an accessory, and support should
route these to the returns process rather than the warranty process. Routing a fit
complaint into a warranty assessment costs three weeks and ends in the same place.

## Ageing

Independent of the warranty and of any impact, a helmet should be replaced after five
years from the date of manufacture, which is moulded into the liner. This is a safety
recommendation and not a coverage period; a five-year-old helmet is not defective, it is
simply past the point at which Velora is willing to make claims about its performance.

## Sizing and returns rather than claims

| Size | Head circumference |
|---|---|
| S | 51 to 55 cm |
| M | 55 to 59 cm |
| L | 59 to 63 cm |

A helmet that does not fit is a return, not a claim, and the accessory return window
applies. Routing a sizing complaint into a warranty assessment costs three weeks and
ends at the same place.

## Determination timelines

Velora acknowledges a claim within 1 working day. Where photographs resolve the
question, a determination follows within 2 working days. Where the helmet must be
returned for assessment, the determination follows within 5 working days of arrival.

## Records and traceability

Every helmet carries a moulded date of manufacture on the liner. That date, not the
purchase date, determines the five-year replacement recommendation, and the two can
differ by many months on a helmet held in stock.

Coverage runs from delivery, not from manufacture.

## Statistics

Roughly 60 % of reported helmet defects resolve on inspection as impact damage, around
25 % as fit complaints, and the remainder as genuine manufacturing defects, of which the
retention dial is the most common.
