# Support Playbook — Warranty Claims

## Order of checks

1. Identify the assembly: battery, drive unit, frame, or accessory. Each has a separate
   instrument and a different period.
2. Establish the delivery date from the order record. Not the order date.
3. For a battery claim, obtain the cycle count from a service partner before any
   assessment. A claim assessed without it will be reopened.
4. Check the affected-list serial numbers from the August 2025 service notice before
   applying the cycle limit.
5. Apply the terms in force at the date of delivery.

## The document that applies

Use the 2025 revision for units delivered on or after 1 March 2025. Units delivered
before that date fall under the previous terms. **If a customer quotes 36 months on a
battery, they are quoting the superseded 2018 document**, which is still findable
online and which support should recognise on sight rather than argue with.

The correct response is to state the terms that apply to their delivery date and to
offer the assessment, not to contest the customer's source.

## Common cases

**"My battery died at 14 months."** Inside the calendar limit. Get the cycle count. If
above 500, the pack is outside the cell coverage period and the claim is declined with
the cycle figure quoted. If below 500 and capacity is below 70 %, it is a claim.

**"I have only had it two years."** Two years is 24 months, which is the boundary, not
inside it. Check the delivery date to the day.

**"The water got in when I washed it."** Not covered. Say so plainly, then offer the
replacement pack price. Do not imply that a claim might succeed on review.

**"Your website said 36 months."** See above. State the applicable terms and the
delivery date.

## Escalation

Escalate to a support lead where the claim value exceeds EUR 800, where the customer
has an open dispute with a payment provider, or where the unit is on the affected list
and the customer is outside 24 months.

## Escalation

Escalate to a support lead where any of the following is true:

- The claim value exceeds EUR 800.
- The customer has opened a dispute with a payment provider.
- The customer states an intention to take legal action or to contact a regulator.
- The case involves a safety concern, however unlikely it appears.
- The unit is on the August 2025 affected list and is outside 24 months.

Escalate to the commercial manager where a discount above 10 % would resolve the case.

## Tone

State the applicable terms plainly and early. A message that hedges for two paragraphs
before declining reads as evasive, and the customer reads the decline anyway.

Never speculate about whether a decision might be reversed on review. Where a decision is
final, say it is final. Where it is genuinely reviewable, name who reviews it and by
when.

## What never appears in a customer-facing message

- Internal margin figures, supplier names, or unit costs.
- Estimates of when a backordered item will ship, beyond the estimated dispatch week on
  the order record.
- Any statement that a warranty period is negotiable.

## Response targets

| Channel | First response | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Email | 1 working day | 5 working days |
| Chat | 3 minutes | same session where possible |
| Phone | — | same call where possible |

Cases open beyond the resolution target are reviewed weekly by the support lead.

## Recording the assessment

Every assessment is recorded on the order record with the measured capacity, the cycle
count, the delivery date, the terms applied, and the outcome. An assessment without the
cycle count will be reopened, because the next agent cannot verify the decision.

## The three questions that resolve most cases

1. What is the delivery date on the order record?
2. What does the cycle counter read?
3. Is the serial on the August 2025 affected list?

Almost every battery case is determined by those three answers. Agents who ask them
first close cases in a single exchange; agents who begin by discussing the customer's
experience of the fault take four.

## Goodwill

Goodwill is a support lead's decision and is recorded as goodwill on the order record,
never as an accepted claim. Recording a goodwill replacement as a claim corrupts the
failure-rate data that the quality team uses, and that data is the input to the supplier
review.

## When the customer is right and the terms are against them

It happens. A customer who was never reached by the August 2025 email and who presents
at month 26 has a reasonable grievance and no contractual entitlement. Say both things.
Escalate. Do not pretend the terms say something they do not, and do not pretend the
grievance is unreasonable.

## Worked examples

**Delivered 12 April 2025. Fails at month 10 with 240 cycles, capacity 64 %.**
2025 terms apply. Inside 24 months, under 500 cycles, capacity below 70 %. Accept.

**Delivered 12 April 2025. Fails at month 10 with 540 cycles, capacity 64 %.**
2025 terms apply. Over 500 cycles. Decline, quoting the cycle figure. Offer the
replacement price.

**Delivered 12 April 2025. Serial on the affected list. Month 20, 610 cycles, 66 %.**
Waiver applies. Cycle count irrelevant. Inside 24 months. Accept.

**Delivered 12 April 2025. Serial on the affected list. Month 26, 610 cycles, 66 %.**
Waiver removes the cycle limit, not the calendar limit. Outside coverage. Escalate: the
customer may never have received the August email.

**Delivered 4 January 2025. Fails at month 30, capacity 62 %.**
Delivered before 1 March 2025, so the previous terms apply: 36 months, no cycle limit.
Month 30 is inside. Accept.

**Delivered 12 April 2025. Water in the pack after pressure washing. Month 6.**
Excluded regardless of period. Decline plainly. Offer the replacement price. Do not
suggest a review might change it.

**Helmet, delivered 12 April 2025, retention dial broken at month 8, no impact.**
Helmet terms, 24 months, manufacturing defect. Request photographs. Likely accept.

**Helmet, visible shell crack after a fall, month 8.**
Impact damage, never covered. Offer the 30 % crash replacement discount.

## The most common agent errors

1. Applying the calendar limit and forgetting the cycle limit, or the reverse.
2. Running the period from the order date instead of the delivery date.
3. Failing to check the affected list before discussing the cycle count.
4. Applying the 2025 terms to a unit delivered before 1 March 2025.
5. Applying the battery period to a drive unit case, or the reverse.

Four of the five are avoided by asking the three questions in order.

## Where to find each document

The terms in force, the superseded terms, the service notice, and the model manuals are
all in the support knowledge base. Where a customer cites a figure that does not match
what you see, ask which document they are reading before contesting the figure. They are
usually reading a real Velora document, just not the current one.
