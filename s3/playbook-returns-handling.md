# Support Playbook — Returns Handling

## Authorising a return

Confirm three things before authorising: the delivery date, the product category, and
that the item is described as resaleable. The window runs from delivery, so an order
placed six weeks ago may still be inside a 30-day window if it was delivered late.

Authorise in the order record. The carrier collects within 5 working days of
authorisation.

## Category matters more than agents expect

The single most common error is applying the bicycle window to an accessory. A rear
rack bought with a bicycle is an accessory and carries the accessory window, even
though it arrived in the same delivery. The window for each part runs from that part's
own delivery date.

## Restocking fees

Batteries carry 15 % and custom builds 25 %. State the fee at the point of
authorisation, never at the point of refund. A fee the customer learns about on the
refund notice becomes a dispute.

## Condition disputes

Where the warehouse reports a returned bicycle as not resaleable, request the photos
before contacting the customer. Roughly a third of these are resolved by looking at the
photograph: road dust reported as damage is common.

## UK specifics

Every UK return needs a returned-goods declaration on the return leg. Send it with the
label. Where it is missing, duty is charged at the border and deducted from the refund,
and the customer will not have expected it.

## Refunds

14 days from receipt at the warehouse. Where a refund is late, the cause is almost
always that the return arrived without its authorisation reference and could not be
matched.

## The authorisation record

An authorisation carries the order reference, the delivery date, the category, the
declared condition, and the agent's name. The carrier collects against the
authorisation; a return sent without one arrives at the warehouse and cannot be matched
to a customer.

Unmatched returns are the single largest cause of a late refund. The warehouse holds
them for 30 days and then contacts the customer through the payment reference if one is
recoverable.

## Deciding the category

Category is determined by what the item is, not by how it was bought. A rack, a helmet
or a spare pack bought in the same order as a bicycle is an accessory or a battery, and
carries its own window and its own fee.

The single most common error is applying the bicycle window to an accessory bought with
a bicycle. The second most common is running the window from the order date rather than
the delivery date.

## Condition assessment

The warehouse photographs every returned bicycle on arrival: drivetrain, both wheel
rims, the frame from both sides, and any reported damage. Request the photographs before
contacting a customer about a condition deduction.

Around a third of reported condition problems resolve on looking at the photograph. Road
dust reported as damage is common; a scuffed pedal reported as a crash is common.

## Deductions

A deduction is never applied without a photographic record. State any deduction at the
point of authorisation where it is foreseeable — restocking fees always are — and never
first on the refund notice.

| Situation | Deduction |
|---|---|
| Missing charger | Replacement cost |
| Missing keys | Replacement cost |
| Battery bought separately | 15 % restocking |
| Custom build | 25 % restocking |
| Damage beyond test-ride wear | Assessed, with photographs |

## Refund mechanics

Refunds go to the original payment method within 14 days of arrival at the warehouse.
A refund cannot be redirected to a different card or account; where the original method
is closed, the payment provider handles it and the timeline extends.

## Partial returns

An order containing several items may be partially returned. Each item carries its own
window from its own delivery date. Where a bundle discount was applied — a helmet bought
with a bicycle at 15 % off — returning the bicycle removes the basis for the discount
and the helmet refund is adjusted accordingly.

State this at authorisation. It is not obvious and it produces a dispute every time it
is discovered on the refund notice.

## UK specifics

Every UK return needs a returned-goods declaration on the return leg, sent with the
label. Where it is missing, duty is charged at the border and deducted, and the customer
will not have expected it.

## When to escalate

Escalate a return to a support lead where the value exceeds EUR 800, where a condition
deduction is disputed, where the item is outside its window but the customer has a
documented reason it should not be, or where the return has been lost in transit.

## Worked examples

**Bicycle delivered 3 March, customer contacts 29 March wanting to return.**
Inside 30 days. Authorise. Velora pays return carriage. No fee.

**Bicycle delivered 3 March, customer contacts 8 April.**
Outside 30 days. Decline the return. Where the customer says they contacted support
earlier, check the case history before declining: an earlier contact inside the window
that was not actioned is Velora's error.

**Rack bought with a bicycle, both delivered 3 March, customer returns the rack 20 March
in the EU.**
Accessory window is 14 days. Outside. Decline. This is the single most disputed case in
the returns process and the reason to state the category window at the point of sale.

**Separately purchased 720 Wh pack, delivered 3 March, returned 12 March.**
Inside the 14-day battery window. Authorise with the 15 % restocking fee stated now, not
on the refund notice.

**Custom build delivered 3 March, returned 10 March.**
Inside 14 days. 25 % restocking fee. Customer pays return carriage.

**UK accessory delivered 3 March, customer contacts 9 March.**
Inside the 7-day UK accessory window. Authorise. Send the returned-goods declaration
with the label.

**Bicycle returned without the charger.**
Deduct the replacement cost of the charger. Tell the customer before processing the
refund.

**Bicycle returned, warehouse reports scratched crank and worn tyres.**
Request the photographs. Road dust and light wear are accepted. Assess against the
photographs and state the deduction with the images attached.

## Timing the customer sees

| Step | Typical elapsed |
|---|---|
| Authorisation | Same day |
| Carrier collection | Within 5 working days |
| Transit to warehouse | 3 to 6 working days, 8 to 12 from the UK |
| Warehouse inspection | 1 to 3 working days |
| Refund issued | Within 14 days of arrival |

A customer asking "where is my refund" three days after collection is asking too early,
and telling them the whole timeline once is faster than answering three times.

## What produces a dispute

In order: a restocking fee first mentioned on the refund notice, a category window the
customer did not know applied, a condition deduction without photographs, and a UK duty
charge the customer did not expect. All four are preventable at the point of
authorisation.
