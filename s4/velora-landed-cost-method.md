# How Velora computes landed cost

Landed cost is the figure against which commercial margin is assessed. It is not the
trade cost, and quoting trade cost as though it were landed cost is the single most
common error in range profitability reviews.

Landed cost per unit is built up as follows.

1. **Trade cost** — the supplier's ex-works price, from the commercial pricing notes.
2. **Inbound freight** — EUR 46 per bicycle, EUR 4 per accessory, on the standard
   sea-plus-road route. Air freight is exceptional and is booked separately.
3. **Duty** — 6 percent of trade cost on bicycles imported from outside the customs
   union. Accessories in the current tariff line attract 4 percent.
4. **Inbound quality control** — EUR 18 per bicycle. This covers the 5-day inbound
   check that follows the supplier's 45-day lead time.

Worked example, Cargo Pro. Trade cost EUR 1 358, freight EUR 46, duty at 6 percent
EUR 81.48, quality control EUR 18. Landed cost EUR 1 503.48 per unit.

Supplier lead time is 45 days contractually, plus the 5-day inbound quality control
step, so 50 days from order to sellable stock. Planning against 45 rather than 50 is
the second most common error in this area.
